AI’s Carbon Footprint — The Energy Race We’re Not Ready For

Exploring how AI data centres are reshaping global electricity demand and emissions.

AI’s Share of Global Electricity

1. The Rise of AI’s Energy Appetite

As AI models grow larger and more complex, the electricity required to train and run them has skyrocketed. In 2024, AI data centers consumed an estimated 85 TWh of electricity — roughly the annual usage of a medium-sized country. Under the “Lift-Off” scenario, demand could triple by 2035.

2. Where Does This Energy Come From?

Despite rapid renewable adoption, the world’s electricity remains dominated by fossil fuels. This means that most of the energy powering AI computation today still comes from coal and gas — reinforcing the link between digital growth and carbon emissions.

3. The Carbon Cost of Intelligence

The carbon intensity of AI depends on where its models are trained. Data centers located in coal-heavy regions like China or the U.S. Midwest produce significantly higher CO₂ per computation than those in Europe or Canada, where renewable grids are stronger.

4. The Unequal Impact

AI’s carbon footprint is not evenly distributed. High-income countries generate the most emissions from AI innovation, while low-income countries experience the downstream effects of climate change. This imbalance highlights the urgent need for equitable and sustainable AI infrastructure.

5. A Call to Design Better Futures

AI’s intelligence may be artificial, but its emissions are real. Smarter algorithms, cleaner grids, and efficient data center design can help bend the curve. Our challenge isn’t just to make models smarter — it’s to power them responsibly.
